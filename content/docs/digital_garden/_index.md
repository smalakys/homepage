Notes on things I've had to understand properly rather than just repeat. Written for
climbers, not for physiologists — jargon is used where it's the right word, and explained
where it isn't obvious.

## Series: Altitude

A six-part series on what altitude does to the human body, in the order the body
encounters it: adaptation first, then its limits, then its failures.

1. **[Acclimatization](acclimatization/)** — how the body adapts to thin air, and why
   it takes as long as it does.
2. **[Altitude and performance](altitude-and-performance/)** — what you lose anyway.
   {{< katex >}}\dot{V}O_{2}\text{max}{{< /katex >}}, pacing, and why acclimatization
   doesn't give your engine back.
3. **[Altitude illness](altitude-illness/)** — AMS, HACE and HAPE: recognising them,
   and the one rule that matters.
4. **[The death zone](death-zone/)** — where acclimatization stops working, and what
   the mortality data actually say.
5. **[Pre-acclimatization](pre-acclimatization/)** — what you can usefully do before
   you leave, and what the industry sells that doesn't work.
6. **[Individual variability](individual-variability/)** — why altitude hits some
   people harder, and how to learn your own profile.
