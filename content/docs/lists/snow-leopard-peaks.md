This page tracks progress toward climbing the five 7000-meter peaks required to earn the historic Snow Leopard mountaineering award.

Established in 1967 in the former Soviet Union, the Snow Leopard award (officially titled "Conqueror of the Highest Mountains of the USSR") is one of high-altitude mountaineering's most famous and rigorous honours. Earning the award requires ascending all five 7000-meter summits located across the Pamir and Tian Shan ranges of Central Asia: Ismoil Somoni Peak (Communism Peak, 7495 m), Jengish Chokusu (Pobeda Peak, 7439 m), Ibn Sina Peak (Lenin Peak, 7134 m), Ozodi Peak (Korzhenevskaya Peak, 7105 m), and Khan Tengri (7010 m). Scattered across Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Kazakhstan, these formidable mountains present extreme high-altitude environments characterized by sub-zero temperatures, intense storms, immense glaciated terrain, and severe avalanche risks. Jengish Chokusu (Pobeda Peak) is particularly renowned as one of the world's most daunting 7000-meter peaks due to its extreme isolation and brutal weather. Pursuing this list represents the ultimate test of expedition self-reliance and high-altitude endurance in former Soviet mountain territory.
