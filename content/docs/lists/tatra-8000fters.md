This page tracks progress toward climbing all 14 summits in the High Tatras that reach or exceed an elevation of 8000 feet (2438 meters).

The High Tatras, forming the highest range of the Carpathian Mountains along the border of Slovakia and Poland, offer a compact yet remarkably fierce alpine environment. By a fun coincidence, the High Tatras contain exactly 14 peaks above 8000 feet, mirroring the world's 14 famous eight-thousand-meter mountains (8000ers). Although lower in elevation than the Alps, these granite peaks feature dramatic vertical relief, sharp ridges, and challenging weather conditions that demand serious mountaineering proficiency. The 8000-foot threshold isolates the range's highest and most prestigious summits, including Gerlachovský štít—the highest peak in the Carpathians—alongside legendary peaks like Lomnický štít, Rysy, and Kriváň. Climbing these peaks involves a mixture of steep scrambles, technical rock routes, and guided ascents deeply rooted in Central European climbing history. This collection provides a dedicated benchmark for systematically exploring the pinnacle objectives of the Tatra mountains.
