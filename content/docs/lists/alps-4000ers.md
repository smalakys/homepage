This page tracks progress toward climbing the 82 official 4000-meter summits of the Alps as defined by the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation (UIAA).

In European mountaineering, reaching 4000 meters (13123 feet) represents the definitive altitude threshold for high-alpine mountaineering. Peaks above this height feature extensive glaciers, steep ice faces, and exposed rock ridges that require classical alpine skills like crampon technique, glacier navigation, and crevasse rescue. In 1994, the UIAA established the definitive list of 82 independent 4000-meter peaks across France, Switzerland, and Italy. Scaling all 82 summits is celebrated as one of the ultimate grand challenges in European alpinism, spanning legendary massifs such as Mont Blanc, Monte Rosa, the Bernese Alps, and the Valais. This list serves as a structured lifelong pursuit to explore the technical variety, natural beauty, and rich climbing history of the Alpine arc.
